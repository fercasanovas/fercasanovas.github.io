How Often Should You Re-Measure AI Visibility?

By CassaInnova · Updated 20 September 2026

There's no fixed interval that's right for every client. A quarterly check is a reasonable default. Add an extra check right after any fix ships, or when a client reports a sudden change.

Answer engines change without notice. A number's shelf life is always shorter than a client expects.

Is There a Standard Interval for Re-Checking AI Visibility?

No industry standard exists. The field itself is young.

The systems being measured change on their own schedule, not the consultant's. What's reasonable depends on how fast the client's category moves, and how much is riding on the number.

What's a Reasonable Default Cadence?

Quarterly is a sensible starting point for most engagements. It's frequent enough to catch a real shift. It's infrequent enough to stay affordable, and to let a fix take effect between checks.

Cadence by situation

SituationReasonable cadence
Steady-state client, no recent changesQuarterly
Right after a technical or footprint fix shipsOne extra check, roughly 2–4 weeks out
A client reports a sudden, noticeable changeAn unscheduled check as soon as practical
A competitive category that moves fastMonthly may be justified — priced accordingly

What Should Trigger a Check Outside the Normal Schedule?

A fix just shipped is the clearest trigger. Checking too soon risks catching an index or model that hasn't caught up yet. Give it a few weeks.

A client noticing something themselves, in a call, is the other common trigger. It deserves an answer sooner than the next scheduled quarter.

  • A fix shipped. Wait a few weeks before checking. Same-day checks often just catch a system that hasn't caught up.
  • A client reports a change. Answer that sooner than the regular schedule. The trigger event itself sets the timeline.
  • A competitor visibly shifts. Worth an off-cycle look, especially if a rival suddenly starts appearing where the client used to.

Why Does a Number's Shelf Life Matter to a Client?

A score reported without its check date reads as current long after it stops being true. These systems move on their own, without warning. Attaching the date and the window every time keeps a client from quoting a stale number back months later, as if nothing could have changed.

Does a Steady Result Over Time Mean You Can Check Less Often?

A little, but not indefinitely. Two or three stable quarters in a row support stretching the interval somewhat.

That's the same reasoning behind how to build a prompt set to test a client's AI visibility. A repeated check is what turns one snapshot into a pattern. Stretching too far just means the next real shift gets caught later than it should.

What Does a Re-Check Actually Involve?

The same questions, run the same way, on the same platforms as the baseline. A re-check that changes the method isn't comparable to what came before.

Note what's different since the last check: a fix shipped, a competitor moved, or nothing obvious at all. That's itself worth recording.

  • Same questions, same platforms, same method, as the baseline. Comparability is the whole point of re-checking at all.
  • Record what changed since last time, on the site and off it, alongside the new result.
  • Report the result with its window, every time, the same discipline described in how to check whether a client is cited.
