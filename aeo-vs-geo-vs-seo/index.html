AEO vs GEO vs SEO: What the Terms Actually Mean

By CassaInnova · Updated 20 September 2026

AEO and GEO are two names for the same practice: getting named inside an AI-generated answer. SEO is the older discipline of ranking a page in a list of search results. They share the same technical foundation but measure different outputs — a list position against a mention inside written text.

What Do AEO and GEO Actually Mean?

Both terms describe the same activity, coined by different corners of the industry around the same time.

  • AEO (answer engine optimization). The term most SEO consultants reach for first, because it echoes existing SEO vocabulary and describes optimizing for a direct answer rather than a ranked list.
  • GEO (generative engine optimization). Surfaced mostly from academic and vendor research, framing the target as generative systems broadly rather than a specific answer box.
  • Neither term is standardized. No industry body owns either definition. Writers and vendors use them inconsistently, sometimes as synonyms, sometimes with invented distinctions that don't hold up.

The overlap is real. A site an AI crawler cannot fetch is invisible for the same underlying reason a search crawler misses it. But the outputs stay distinct — one is a rank, the other is a mention a reader has to notice inside a paragraph.

Is AEO Just SEO Rebranded?

Partly, and it's worth saying which part.

  • What carries over. Crawlability, indexability, clear entity signals, and structured data all matter to both disciplines, for the same reasons.
  • What doesn't map cleanly. Backlink-driven authority models and rank-tracking tools were built around a list of ten results; a generated answer often surfaces only one or two sources at once.
  • What's genuinely new. The output is prose a reader has to read, not a scannable list — so being present and being described accurately are two separate things to check.

Which Term Should a Consultant Use With a Client?

  • Use whichever term the client already used to ask the question. If they said "ChatGPT," answer in those words before introducing AEO or GEO.
  • Lead with the plain description, not the acronym. "Whether AI tools name you when someone asks" lands better in a sales call than either label.
  • Don't present the acronym choice as a methodology decision. It isn't one — the measurement underneath is what matters, not which of the two interchangeable labels sits on top of it.
