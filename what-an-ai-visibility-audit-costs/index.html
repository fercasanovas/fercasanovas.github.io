What Should an AI Visibility Audit Cost?

By CassaInnova · Last updated 16 September 2026

A single-vertical AI visibility audit — one brand, one domain, one competitive set, measured across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Overviews — typically runs $1,500 to $3,000 as a one-time engagement. Below that, a tool is standing in for a person; above it, you're largely paying for an agency's overhead on top of the same underlying diagnosis.

What Determines Where in the $1,500–$3,000 Range a Quote Lands?

Two things move a quote inside that range, and neither is how well a consultant negotiates.

Scope size is the first: a smaller, fixed question set focused on one competitive set sits near the bottom of the range; a larger battery — more questions, deeper competitor coverage, per-angle diagnosis rather than one composite — sits near the top. The floor and ceiling aren't arbitrary; they track how many hours the underlying measurement actually takes, because every question gets asked more than once, on separate days, and read by a person rather than scraped by a script.

Existing information is the second: a client whose category phrase, competitor list, and priority pages are already documented costs a consultant less setup time than a brand-new prospect who needs a full scoping conversation first — and that difference should show up honestly in the quote rather than get absorbed silently into the fee.

Why Doesn't an AI Visibility Audit Cost $250 Like the Automated Tools?

An automated scan and a consultant-run audit are not the same product, even when they sit on the same comparison page.

A scan at $250–$500 answers a narrower question — usually a single snapshot from a single account — and produces a number with nothing behind it a client can independently check. What separates a $1,500 audit from a $250 tool report isn't branding; it's a logged evidence trail: the actual questions asked, the actual dates, the actual answers returned, saved and handed over — so a skeptical client, or a skeptical consultant reviewing someone else's work, can verify any single finding by hand instead of trusting a dashboard's math.

Why Doesn't an AI Visibility Audit Cost $8,000–$15,000 Like an AEO Agency?

An agency retainer in that range typically bundles the same underlying diagnosis with strategy work, implementation, and ongoing monitoring — a program, not a single measurement.

A one-time audit priced at $1,500–$3,000 is deliberately scoped to stop at the diagnosis: a scored result, a prioritized fix list, and the evidence behind both. It doesn't include an agency's overhead, and it doesn't include doing the fix-list work for the client. Those are legitimate services, and some consultants and agencies sell them well — they're just a different, larger engagement than the one this price band describes.

What Does the Fee for an AI Visibility Audit Actually Cover?

The fee for an AI visibility audit covers hours, mostly, and they don't compress much no matter who's doing the work.

Every question in the set gets asked more than once, on separate days, across four platforms — because a single answer from a system that changes over time is a data point, not a finding, and scoring off one run reports a coin flip as a fact. A meaningful share of the client's own site also gets checked directly: can the relevant crawlers actually read the pages, is the brand described consistently across the web, is there a factual footprint an answer engine could plausibly be drawing an answer from. The result is then assembled into something a client can act on — a written, ordered list of what to fix first, not a raw data dump.

None of that compresses to an hour of work, which is the actual reason the floor of this range sits where it does rather than lower.

Is a Monthly Retainer the Right Model for This Work?

A monthly retainer is usually the wrong model for this work, and the arithmetic is the reason rather than a stylistic preference.

A single measurement has a natural cadence — roughly every ninety days is enough time for a fix list to actually ship and for a re-measurement to mean something. Priced monthly against what an entry-level ongoing search program typically charges (commonly $1,000–$2,500 a month), a single audit's value works out to a few hundred dollars a month once annualized — below what that market segment expects for a standing retainer, because most of what a retainer buyer is actually paying for is work between measurements, not the measurement itself.

The more defensible model is to quote a baseline audit, then quote the follow-up measurement separately, at roughly the same fee, when it's actually due — rather than packaging a quarterly instrument as if it were a monthly service.
