About CassaInnova and This Site

By CassaInnova · Updated 20 September 2026

CassaInnova publishes the AI Visibility Audit Kit: a documented method for measuring whether AI answer engines name a brand. This site explains that method openly, so a consultant can judge the thinking before buying anything, and so the claims here can be checked.

Who Writes This

Fernando — a software engineer with more than fifteen years building software, now building tools for the AI era.

Not a career SEO. That is worth saying plainly, because this site is written for people who are.

What an engineer brings to this problem is the habit of treating an unreliable system as a measurement problem rather than a mystery:

  • Ask the same question more than once, because one answer from a non-deterministic system is a sample, not a fact.
  • Write down the raw result, not a summary of it.
  • Publish the method so someone else can run it and disagree with you.

What an engineer does not bring is fifteen years of client work in search. A consultant reading this has more of that than I do, which is exactly why the kit is a documented instrument rather than advice. The method has to stand on its own, because it cannot lean on a résumé.

What This Site Actually Claims

Being precise about this matters more here than anywhere else. The product is a measurement method, and a method that overclaims is worthless.

What is claimed:

  • A documented, repeatable measurement of whether a brand is named in AI answers.
  • An evidence trail behind every finding — the question asked, the platform, the date, the response.
  • A scoring method a consultant can check their own arithmetic against before using it on a client.

What is never claimed:

  • That any brand will be cited, ranked, or recommended. The platforms do not disclose how sources are chosen, and they change without notice. Nobody can honestly promise this.
  • That the method makes AI answers predictable. It does not. It measures a system that returns different answers to the same question.
  • That a tool is never worth buying. Only that the first audit needs none.

What Has Been Measured Here

This site runs the kit's own technical checks against itself. A method for auditing sites is worth more from a site that passes its own audit:

  • Crawler access — a named AI crawler is served the same page a person is.
  • Directives — robots.txt names the crawlers this method measures, and the llms.txt is accurate or it goes.
  • Structure and markup — one heading per page, self-referencing canonicals, declared entity data.

The script that does it is in the public repository, so this is checkable rather than asserted.

Original measurement published here: fifty B2B SaaS sites checked for AI crawler access, reported in aggregate, with the sample's limits stated and the code available to re-run.

How This Site Is Built

Plain HTML, with no tracking, no cookies and no analytics. Nothing here follows you.

That is partly principle and partly consistency. A site about being readable by machines should not be heavy for people.

Pages follow a published standard. The answer comes first, sentences stay short enough to read once, and the structure is built for scanning rather than for reading straight through.
