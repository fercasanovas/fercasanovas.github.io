How Do You Add a New Service to an Agency's Pricing?

By CassaInnova · Updated 20 September 2026

Add it as a fixed-fee line beside your existing technical audit, not folded into an hourly rate or an existing retainer. Price it in its own band — roughly $1,500 to $3,000 — and scope it identically every time. Treat the first few sales as building a track record, not permanent discounts.

Where Does This Sit Relative to What You Already Sell?

  • Beside the technical audit, not inside it. It's a related but separate instrument — different platforms, different evidence, a different question being answered.
  • Not folded into an hourly rate. This method is priced by what's delivered, not by a clock — the whole point is a fixed, comparable instrument.
  • Not a line item inside an existing retainer, at least not at first. Should you sell it as a retainer or a one-off covers why the arithmetic argues against that by default.

How Do You Price It Without Undercutting Your Own Audit Line?

The market has three tiers already, and the new line only works if it stays inside its own.

  • Don't price toward the cheap tier. A client comparing you to a $250–$500 scan should see the evidence appendix, not a discount.
  • Don't price toward the agency tier either, unless you're actually delivering strategy and implementation alongside it. The diagnosis alone belongs in the middle band.

Should You Discount the First Few Times You Sell It?

A short, deliberate step-down is defensible. An open-ended one isn't.

  • A real reason to step down once: it's your first time running the method in that vertical. Part of what you get back is a finished, showable result for the next prospect.
  • A real reason to step down again: something non-cash coming back in writing — a named reference, a case study, an introduction.
  • Not a reason: "they asked," or "it's new so it should be cheaper." The scope doesn't get smaller because the service is younger.

How Do You Introduce It to Existing Clients Without It Reading as an Upsell?

  • Tie it to a question they've already asked. If a client raised "are we showing up in ChatGPT" on a call, that's the opening — not a cold pitch.
  • Scope it the same way for an existing client as a new one. The facts you already hold about them can justify a small step-down, but not a different instrument.
  • Keep the fixed scope visible. Same platforms, same counts, same evidence — the existing client is buying the same rigor a new prospect would.

What Shouldn't Change Once the Line Exists?

  • The scope stays fixed, regardless of who's asking or what they can afford. A smaller version is a different, unpriced instrument, not a discount.
  • The re-measurement stays scheduled at roughly ninety days, at the same price point, every time — that repeat sale is where a new service line starts paying for itself.

Once it's priced, the number itself should match what an AI visibility audit should cost rather than a figure invented to fit an existing rate card.
